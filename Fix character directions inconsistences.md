# Fix Character Direction & Head Orientation Inconsistencies

## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

In multi-clip continuous dramatic video generation (e.g. Story Videos mode with Seedance 2.0), consecutive clips often suffer from jarring **"jump-cut orientation snaps"** or **"puff switches"**. 

### Real Case Study (Job `102b2d9f441f49aa8c75f953ef7b4117`):
* **Prompt:** A young woman (Elena Hart) resting in her Georgian townhouse bedroom; a tiny fairy (Aurelia) emerges from a swirl of golden light.
* **Clip 1 (`https://tempfile.aiquickdraw.com/seedance/1791026630602-cgsocjtjz7w.mp4`):**
  * Elena is seated on the edge of the walnut bed, with her head and body tilted slightly toward screen-right / camera-left.
* **Clip 2 (`https://tempfile.aiquickdraw.com/seedance/1791026810589-yt1iipl4mnp.mp4`):**
  * Elena suddenly appears tilted in the opposite direction (facing screen-left) across the cut without any smooth physical turn.

---

## 2. Root Cause Analysis

Analysis of the exact scripts generated by OpenAI and passed to Seedance reveals three primary causes:

### A. Camera Aim Drift (Unanchored Camera Placement)
* **Clip 1 Prompt:** Camera placed at the west wall desk, aiming **North-East directly toward the bed** (*"toward Elena seated centre frame on the foot edge of the walnut bed"*).
* **Clip 2 Prompt:** Camera placed at the west wall desk, aiming **East directly toward the sash window** (*"looking across the unobstructed floor toward the sash window"*).
* In cinematography, shifting camera aim by 45°–90° to face a different wall fundamentally alters perspective on a character sitting on the bed. Because the camera target changed, the character's angle relative to the lens was completely scrambled.

### B. Conflicting 2D Screen Composition Directives
* **Clip 1 Prompt:** Elena was centered (*"seated centre frame"*), resolving on a 3/4 profile illuminated by the east window. Seedance naturally oriented her facing toward the light (screen-right).
* **Clip 2 Prompt:** OpenAI explicitly commanded a new 2D layout:
  > *"resolving on a medium-wide profile frame with the hovering light centre-left and Elena seated frame right"*
* To satisfy the 2D layout constraint of having the **light on the left (`centre-left`)** and **Elena on the right (`frame right`)**, Seedance's diffusion model was forced to flip Elena's face and body to face **screen-left** so she could look at the light.

### C. Absence of 3D Skeletal Tracking & Screen Direction Memory
* Video diffusion models (Seedance 2.0, Sora, Kling) do not operate on fixed 3D skeletal rigs. They generate pixels conditioned on 2D composition descriptions.
* The system's continuity memory (`_state_so_far`) recorded:
  * `posture: "sitting"`
  * `position: "seated on bed"`
  * `facing: "south toward open floor"`
* However, it **did not track head tilt angle, eye-line vector, or screen profile** (e.g. *"head tilted 15° left, 3/4 profile facing screen-right"*). Consequently, OpenAI did not carry the exact head orientation forward, leaving Seedance free to generate whatever orientation fit the new prompt.

### D. Frame 0.0s Snapping (Lack of Transition Animation)
* Clip 2 started with Elena already staring at the light from `0.0s`. Because diffusion models render frame 0 based on the starting prompt, her head orientation instantly snapped rather than smoothly rotating from her previous resting pose.

---

## 3. The 4-Pillar Architectural Solution

To eliminate head-snapping and orientation inconsistencies across conversations, table scenes, and solo moments, the pipeline requires four interconnected pillars:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 1: Cinematic Camera Policy (HOLD vs CUT)        │
│ - Continuous scene: Camera stays 100% LOCKED           │
│ - Angle change: Only for motivated cinematic cuts      │
└──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                           │
┌──────────────────────────▼─────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 2: Head Pose & Eye-Line Continuity Memory       │
│ - Track `head_tilt` and `screen_orientation`           │
│ - Starting pose of Clip N+1 MUST EQUAL end pose of N   │
└──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                           │
┌──────────────────────────▼─────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 3: Smooth Motion Timing (No 0.0s Snaps)       │
│ - Head turns must be explicit timeline action steps    │
│   e.g. "[1.0s–2.5s] smoothly rotates head to right"   │
└──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                           │
┌──────────────────────────▼─────────────────────────────┐
│ Pillar 4: Seedance Temporal Anchor Directives          │
│ - Prompt injection strictly locking 0.0s starting pose │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### Pillar 1: Cinematic Camera Policy (`[SAME SETUP]` vs `[ANGLE CUT]`)

In multi-speaker or continuous scene generation:
1. **Default Rule — Locked Camera (`[SAME SETUP]`):**
   * If characters are conversing in the same room or at the same table across consecutive clips, the camera position, focal length, and framing must remain identical:
     > *"Camera setup is locked in the identical position and focal length as Clip N. Frame composition remains stable with Character A screen-left and Character B screen-right."*
2. **Motivated Cuts Only (`[ANGLE CUT]`):**
   * The camera only switches when a deliberate cinematic cut is required (e.g., cutting from a two-shot to a close-up reaction shot, or an over-the-shoulder reverse angle).
   * **The 180-Degree Rule:** Even during a cut, the imaginary axis of action must never be crossed. Characters must maintain their screen vector (the speaker on the left must still gaze toward screen-right in close-ups so eye-lines match).

---

### Pillar 2: Explicit Head Pose & Eye-Line Continuity Memory

The character blocking schema must track fine-grained head and eye orientation:
* `screen_profile`: (e.g. `3/4 profile facing screen-right`, `profile facing screen-left`, `frontal with head tilted 10° left`).
* `head_tilt`: (e.g. `tilted slightly left`, `upright`, `chin down`).
* `eyeline`: (e.g. `gazing across table at speaker at seated eye height`).

#### The Golden Continuity Rule:
> **The starting head pose, body tilt, and gaze of Clip $N+1$ at 0.0s MUST be an exact 1:1 match to the ending state of Clip $N$.**

---

### Pillar 3: Smooth Motion Timing (No Snaps at 0.0s)

If a character needs to shift attention (e.g., Character A stops speaking and Character C on the other side of the table starts speaking):
* The character **cannot** start Clip $N+1$ already facing Character C!
* The head turn must be explicitly written into the `action_steps` timeline:
  * **[0.0s to 1.0s]:** Elena maintains her exact starting posture and head tilt from the previous cut (head tilted left, gazing toward Person A).
  * **[1.0s to 2.5s]:** Elena smoothly rotates her head and shifts her gaze across the table toward Person C.
  * **[2.5s to 5.0s]:** She settles into the new orientation, listening attentively.

---

### Pillar 4: Seedance Temporal Anchor Directives in `build_multi_prompt`

When compiling the final text prompt for Seedance:
* Inject an explicit temporal continuity anchor at the start of the prompt:
  > *"CONTINUITY ANCHOR: At 0.0s, character body posture, head tilt, and screen direction strictly match the preceding shot: [Character: seated frame-left, head tilted slightly left, facing screen-right]. No sudden snapping or mirroring of face direction at the cut; any change in gaze must be executed as a smooth, continuous turn during the action timeline."*

Diffusion models pay close attention to negative temporal phrasing that binds frame `0.0s` to a specific anchor.
